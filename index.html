    LEVEL UP YOUR BRAIN

    🧠 Science Skills Academy

    1. Ask a testable question

    A good science question can be investigated by observing or measuring something.

    Example:

    Does the shape of paper change how quickly it falls?

    2. Make a fair test

    Change only one main thing at a time. Keep other important conditions the same.

    Example:

    Use the same paper size and same drop height, but change its shape.

    3. Repeat your trials

    One result can be unusual. Repeating a test helps you notice patterns and makes your evidence stronger.

    4. Observe, don't assume

    An observation is what you notice or measure. An explanation is your idea about why it happened.

    5. Use a graph

    A graph helps people compare results quickly. Your graph should use your own recorded measurements.

    6. Be honest about limits

    Say what your experiment can show—and what it cannot show. Science improves when we are honest.
